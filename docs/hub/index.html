Notes

1. ICMJE

The text came from a fetch tool's extraction of the Introduction paragraph, returned as three verbatim sentences, not a raw HTML read. If the slide shows a full sentence, re-check the live page. Full third sentence as returned: "Cite only directly pertinent references, and do not include data or conclusions from the work being reported."

2. STROBE

Read from the official PDF. Item 1 is title/abstract, 2 is background/rationale, 3 is objectives.

3. CONSORT 2025

Items are 6 (background and rationale) and 7 (objectives), not 5 and 6, because CONSORT 2025 added an open-science block (items 2-5) before Introduction. Wording: item 6 "Scientific background and rationale"; item 7 "Specific objectives related to benefits and harms". CONSORT 2010 used 2a and 2b, so do not mix numbering. PMC11996237 is the PLoS Med publication of the same statement.

4. Swales CARS

Source level is secondary (a university writing blog, and a review that cites Swales). A UMass Writing Center page returned HTTP 403. Search snippets (not opened) give the steps under each move. Bahadoran 2018 (opened) says CARS "follows three moves including establishing a territory (the situation), establishing a niche (the problem), and occupying a niche (the solution)", citing Swales and Najjar and a 2011 Swales reissue, not the 1990 book directly. No source I found says one move is most often omitted. Do not claim it on the slide.

5. Practical guides

  • Cals & Kotz 2013: funnel shape. Length "typically not more than 10-15%" of full word count. Develop "four to five paragraphs". Checklist sections: general background; what is known and unknown; primary research question; study aim and design. Not a full review; leave comparisons for the Discussion. Present tense for established facts, past tense or present perfect for findings not considered established. End with research question or hypothesis plus brief design. Separate primary from secondary aims. Cite only truly relevant references.
  • Bahadoran 2018: funnel or inverted pyramid. "usually consists of 2 - 3 ... and sometimes 4 paragraphs" (known, unknown/gap, hypothesis/question, sometimes approach). Length: "not exceed one double-spaced typed page", about 250-300 words, sometimes 500-600; 10-15% of the manuscript; over two-thirds of the Results length is probably too long. Paragraph 3-4 sentences or 75-150 words. Present tense for established knowledge, past tense for results being reported. Should not include results or conclusions. Objectives "no more than two sentences". Avoid sweeping generalisations in the first sentence. Pitfalls include too much general information and detailed review of earlier work. Most of the numbers are secondhand from sources it cites, so attribute as "Bahadoran et al., summarising".
  • Bavdekar 2015: abstract only. Rationale and main purpose; "focused, succinct and crisp"; mistakes are extensive literature review and unclear hypothesis/objectives. Length, paragraphs and tense are NOT verified (full text not opened).
  • Word and paragraph guidance found: Bahadoran (250-300 words, 2-3 paragraphs) and Cals & Kotz (10-15%, 4-5 paragraphs). They differ on paragraph number; present both.

6. Lifetime risk

Flores-Mireles 2015 gives "150 million people each year worldwide" but the word "lifetime" does not occur in the full text I read. Use Foxman 2002. The same abstract says "Nearly 1 in 3 women will have had at least 1 episode of UTI requiring antimicrobial therapy by the age of 24 years." Figures are US-based and from 1990s data; flag that for an Indian audience.

7. E. coli and ESBL

  • Flores-Mireles full text: "The most common causative agent for both uncomplicated and complicated UTIs is uropathogenic E[scherichia coli]" (text cut at the bracket by my extraction; it is that sentence). It also says E. coli and K. pneumoniae "have both acquired plasmids encoding extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs)".
  • IDSA: the 2024 version was not opened. The live page is the 2026 guidance. A separate IDSA complicated-UTI guideline page (https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/complicated-urinary-tract-infections/, dated 17 Jul 2025) was fetched and does not mention ESBL. For "few oral options" the best-supported wording is the ICMR data below plus IDSA's nitrofurantoin point, not a guideline sentence saying "limited".
  • Pitout & Laupland: abstract only; supports ESBL E. coli as a community UTI cause and carbapenems as drugs of choice for severe infections.

8. ICMR AMRSN

Latest found: Annual Report 2024, 8th edition. Direct curl was blocked; WebFetch downloaded the PDF and I extracted text with pdftotext. I did not browse the icmr.gov.in/amr index, so a newer file is not ruled out. Text read directly:

  • 99,027 culture-positive isolates in 2024; 26,562 from urine.
  • Clinical relevance box: "Fairly good susceptibility was observed for nitrofurantoin and fosfomycin", which remain the drug of choice for cystitis.
  • Urinary E. coli: piperacillin-tazobactam susceptibility fell from 73.7% (2017) to 55.9% (2024); cefotaxime susceptibility stayed 24.5% to 25.8%.
  • E. coli from all samples EXCEPT urine and faeces: 10.7% susceptible to ciprofloxacin and 15.8% to levofloxacin; the report links this to "widespread" ESBL production. Do not present these as urinary figures.
  • Caveat: ICMR 2020 report (search snippet only, not opened) says network data come from tertiary hospitals and do not reflect community AMR. State it.

Blocked or unreachable

  • pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov via WebFetch (cookie wall); the PubMed tool was used instead.
  • umass.edu writing centre (403).
  • Swales 1990 and 2004 books: not accessible.

What this means for a slide

Title evidence: shorter, result-stating titles are modestly associated with more citations, but the effect is small, observational and mixed, so teach "informative, design-labelled, no abbreviations" (ICMJE, reporting guidelines) rather than a magic word count. AI references: these rates (about 18% to 55% fabricated, 91% for Bard) apply to specific older models and prompts from 2023-24 and may not hold for current models, so present them as "this has happened, verify every reference", not as current rates.
