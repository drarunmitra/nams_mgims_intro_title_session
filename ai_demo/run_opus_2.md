**Introduction**

Urinary tract infections (UTIs) are among the most common bacterial infections in both community and hospital settings. Uropathogenic *Escherichia coli* causes most uncomplicated and many complicated UTIs (1). Because UTIs are so frequent, they account for a large share of empirical antibiotic prescribing, so resistance in urinary *E. coli* directly affects routine clinical care.

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is a major global health threat. In 2019, an estimated 1.27 million deaths were directly attributable to bacterial AMR and 4.95 million were associated with it. *E. coli* was the leading pathogen in deaths attributable to resistance (2). Extended-spectrum beta-lactamase (ESBL) production is a key mechanism. ESBLs hydrolyse penicillins, third-generation cephalosporins and aztreonam, and are often carried on plasmids with genes for resistance to fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides and co-trimoxazole (3). This narrows the oral options for outpatients with UTI and increases reliance on carbapenems. Heavier carbapenem use in turn drives carbapenem resistance.

India has a high burden of ESBL-producing Enterobacterales. Contributing factors include high per-capita antibiotic consumption, over-the-counter sale of antibiotics, inadequate sanitation, and a large, fragmented private health sector (4). Surveillance by the Indian Council of Medical Research's Antimicrobial Resistance Research and Surveillance Network shows high resistance to third-generation cephalosporins and fluoroquinolones in *E. coli* isolated from urine and other specimens (5). However, network data come mainly from tertiary centres. ESBL prevalence varies widely between regions, levels of care and patient groups, and data from many states and from community settings remain limited.

Local antibiograms are needed to guide empirical therapy, to update institutional antibiotic policies, and to support antimicrobial stewardship under India's National Action Plan on AMR. This study therefore aimed to estimate the prevalence of ESBL production among *E. coli* urinary isolates at [study setting], describe their susceptibility to commonly used antibiotics, and identify factors associated with ESBL carriage.
