Empirical UTI treatment in many Indian settings relies on oral agents such as fluoroquinolones, nitrofurantoin and fosfomycin. International guidelines recommend culture-guided therapy where resistance is common.[5] Without local resistance surveillance, empirical choices risk treatment failure, prolonged illness and further selection pressure.

Contemporary, well-characterised data on ESBL-producing E. coli in urinary isolates across Indian clinical and community settings, including antibiotic co-resistance profiles and risk factors, are still needed. This study addresses that gap by estimating the prevalence of ESBL production among E. coli urinary isolates, describing their antimicrobial susceptibility patterns, and identifying factors associated with ESBL carriage. The findings are intended to inform empirical prescribing and infection-control policy.
