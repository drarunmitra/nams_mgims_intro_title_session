**Introduction**

Urinary tract infections (UTIs) are among the most common bacterial infections. They occur in the community and in hospitals, and they lead to a large share of antibiotic prescriptions worldwide. Uropathogenic *Escherichia coli* causes most uncomplicated and many complicated UTIs (1). As a result, resistance patterns in urinary *E. coli* largely decide whether first-line empirical treatment works.

Extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs) are enzymes that break down penicillins, third-generation cephalosporins and aztreonam. The genes that code for them often sit on plasmids, which also carry resistance to fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides and co-trimoxazole. ESBL-producing Enterobacterales have spread from hospitals into the community, with the CTX-M family dominating (2). This leaves few oral options for outpatients and pushes clinicians towards carbapenems, which in turn speeds up carbapenem resistance.

The global burden is large. An estimated 1.27 million deaths in 2019 were directly attributable to bacterial antimicrobial resistance, and *E. coli* was the leading pathogen in deaths associated with resistance (3). South Asia carries a disproportionate share of this burden. In India, several factors drive resistance: high antibiotic use, over-the-counter sales, poor sanitation, and weak regulation of antibiotics in human and animal use (4). Surveillance by the Indian Council of Medical Research shows persistently high resistance to third-generation cephalosporins and fluoroquinolones among *E. coli* isolates, including those from urine (5).

However, most Indian data come from tertiary-care referral centres, so the patients sampled are skewed towards severe, previously treated infections. Few studies describe ESBL prevalence, co-resistance and associated factors in urinary isolates from district or secondary-level settings, where most patients are seen and empirical prescribing is routine. Local antibiograms are needed to guide empirical therapy and stewardship. This study therefore estimated the proportion of ESBL production among urinary *E. coli* isolates, described their co-resistance profiles, and identified factors associated with ESBL carriage.
