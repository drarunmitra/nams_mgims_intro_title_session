Introduction

Urinary tract infections (UTIs) are among the most common bacterial infections worldwide and a leading reason for outpatient antibiotic prescribing. Escherichia coli causes most community-acquired and many hospital-acquired UTIs, and it is also a major reservoir of resistance to third-generation cephalosporins. Extended-spectrum β-lactamase (ESBL) production, most often by CTX-M-type enzymes, hydrolyses penicillins and oxyimino-cephalosporins. This leaves carbapenems, fosfomycin, nitrofurantoin and pivmecillinam as the main remaining options for many infections, and some of these are limited to lower urinary tract infection.

Antimicrobial resistance is a leading cause of morbidity and death globally, and the burden of resistant Gram-negative infections is highest in low- and middle-income countries.(1) India is widely regarded as a high-burden setting. Reported ESBL prevalence among E. coli urinary isolates from Indian hospitals varies considerably by region, patient population and study period, and in several studies exceeds half of isolates.(2,4) Community-onset ESBL carriage is also increasingly documented, so empirical therapy guided only by hospital data may misjudge the risk for outpatients.(3,5)

Established risk factors include prior antibiotic exposure, urinary catheterisation, diabetes, recent hospitalisation, older age and female sex. Self-medication, empirical prescribing without culture, and limited diagnostic stewardship in both public and private sectors are thought to contribute. Urine culture is often unavailable or delayed at primary care level, which makes local surveillance data essential for guiding empirical treatment and national policy.

Data from community and peripheral settings in India remain sparse. Many studies are single-centre, use convenience sampling, and do not report molecular characterisation or patient-level risk factors. Well-designed studies that describe the prevalence, phenotypic and genotypic profile, and clinical and community correlates of ESBL-producing E. coli in urinary isolates across diverse settings are therefore needed. This study aims to estimate the prevalence of ESBL production among E. coli urinary isolates in India, describe their antimicrobial susceptibility patterns, and identify factors associated with ESBL carriage.
